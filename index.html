EPISODE I — THE PRICE OF A DEMAND

It is a period of continuous contention. Across six years, thirteen movements have pressed the Indian state from thirteen directions, some for a single afternoon, some for four hundred days.

They have blocked highways, halted freight, emptied hospitals and fasted in sub-zero air. A few have won. Most have not.

Conventional wisdom holds that the biggest and longest movements prevail. The record in this archive says otherwise. What separates a repeal from a lathi charge is the price of the thing demanded, and which institution alone was able to pay it.

This terminal measures the pressure applied, the force returned, and the venue where power finally moved. Its weights are arbitrary and it says so on every screen.

Thirteen purposively selected high-salience cases, not a sample. ACLED logged roughly 20,600 demonstrations in India in 2025 alone. Nothing here estimates a national rate, and the intensity index is constructed by this dashboard rather than measured.
